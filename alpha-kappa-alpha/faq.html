Everything You Wanted to Know

What do the letters AKA stand for?

Alpha Kappa Alpha (ΑΚΑ) - and the founders picked the very FIRST letter of the Greek alphabet twice. First on purpose.

Was AKA really the first?

Yes! Founded in 1908, Alpha Kappa Alpha is the first Greek-letter sorority founded by African American women. Everybody else followed their lead.

What are AKA’s colors?

Salmon pink and apple green - soft, pretty, and instantly recognizable.

Why the ivy leaf?

Ivy climbs, clings and stays green all year - it endures. Just like an AKA. The flower is the pink tea rose.

What does “skee-wee” mean?

It’s the famous AKA greeting call! Real members earned the right to use it - fans like us just admire it.

What is “Service to All Mankind”?

The sorority’s purpose: help EVERYONE, everywhere. That’s the whole point of the pink and green.

Who are the founders and incorporators?

Nine founders started AKA in 1908, and seven incorporators made it legally permanent in 1913 - sixteen women total, honored every year.

How big is AKA?

Over 360,000 members in more than 1,000 chapters around the world.

Who is Pink Ivy?

Ahriyah Moore - the very first accepted member of the D9Legacy AKA chapter at Magnolia Crown University. A legend in our universe.

Do AKAs only do service?

Service is the mission, but sisterhood is the fun: Founders Day, the Boule, step shows, and friends for life.

Is this the real Alpha Kappa Alpha website?

Nope! This is Josh's D9Legacy fan chapter - a kid-friendly universe built for fun and learning. The real website is aka1908.com. We just love AKA here.

Can kids join?

Kids can join the D9Legacy fan chapter right now - take the quiz! The real Alpha Kappa Alpha is for college students and adults, so school comes first.
