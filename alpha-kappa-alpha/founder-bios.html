The Real Women

Sixteen Stories, One Sisterhood

Alpha Kappa Alpha was built by sixteen real women - nine founders in 1908 and seven incorporators who made it official in 1913. These biographies are our kid-sized fan tribute to them. Every one of them was a real person with a real story, and most of them were teachers - women who changed the world from the front of a classroom.

1908

The Nine Founders

&

Ethel Hedgeman Lyle

Founder (1908) - The Dreamer

The one who had the idea first. As a student at Howard University, Ethel imagined a sisterhood of smart, kind, hard-working young women - and then she actually built it. Friends called her the “guiding light” of the founding. She became a teacher and lived to see her dream grow far beyond one campus.

&

Anna Easter Brown

Founder (1908) - The Teacher

An honor student from North Carolina who believed education was the best gift you could give somebody. She spent about 50 years teaching young people in West Virginia, North Carolina and New Jersey - half a century of changing lives one classroom at a time.

&

Beulah Elizabeth Burke

Founder (1908) - The Scholar

She studied Latin, Greek, German and economics at a time when very few women - and very few Black Americans - got that chance. Beulah helped choose the sorority's name and its famous salmon pink and apple green colors. She went on to become a teacher and a school principal.

&

Lillie Burke

Founder (1908) - The Sister

Beulah's sister and a beloved English teacher in Washington, D.C. public schools. She was so good at teaching that she ended up training other teachers too - a teacher of teachers.

&

Marjorie Hill

Founder (1908) - The Youngest

One of the youngest of the nine. She graduated from Howard in 1908 and became a teacher. Her own story was short - she passed away just one year after the founding - but the sisterhood she helped start is still going strong more than a century later.

&

Margaret Flagg Holmes

Founder (1908) - The Record Keeper

A teacher and librarian who helped keep the young sorority's records and history safe. Because of women like her, the AKA story was never lost - we can still read about it today.

&

Lavinia Norman

Founder (1908) - The Long Legacy

She taught English, Latin, French and drama in West Virginia for more than 40 years. Lavinia lived to be 100 years old - the last living founder - and got to watch Alpha Kappa Alpha grow into a worldwide sisterhood.

&

Lucy Diggs Slowe

Founder (1908) - The Champion

Brains AND backhands. In 1917 Lucy became the first African American woman to win a national sports championship, taking the American Tennis Association singles title. Later she became the very first Dean of Women at Howard University.

&

Marie Woolfolk Taylor

Founder (1908) - The Builder

From Atlanta, Georgia. Marie helped carry Alpha Kappa Alpha beyond Howard's campus, working to start one of the sorority's very first new chapters in Chicago - the first steps of a sisterhood that now circles the globe.

1913

The Seven Incorporators

&

Norma Elizabeth Boyd

Incorporator (1913) - The Official Maker

In 1913 Norma led the work to legally incorporate Alpha Kappa Alpha, making it official under the law so it could grow and last forever. She taught in Washington, D.C. schools for more than 30 years and spent her life standing up for children.

&

Ethel Jones Mowbray

Incorporator (1913) - The Leader

One of the sorority's earliest leaders. She helped guide Alpha Kappa Alpha from a brand-new campus club into a real movement, then became a teacher and school principal in Kansas City.

&

Alice P. Murray

Incorporator (1913) - The Musician

A gifted musician who loved sharing her talent. Alice helped the young sorority through its first big years and spent her career as a teacher, bringing learning - and a little music - to her students.

&

Sarah Meriweather Nutter

Incorporator (1913) - The Community Builder

A teacher who carried AKA's ideals into new towns across West Virginia. She and her husband, a lawyer who fought discrimination, spent their lives building stronger schools and stronger communities.

&

Joanna Berry Shields

Incorporator (1913) - The Grower

Joanna helped the young sorority welcome new members and grow beyond its first home. She taught school for decades in North Carolina and New York, planting AKA's values in every classroom.

&

Carrie Snowden

Incorporator (1913) - The Math Mind

The numbers person. Carrie loved mathematics at Howard and brought that sharp, careful thinking to the young sorority. She later became a teacher, passing her love of learning on to her students.

&

Harriet Josephine Terry

Incorporator (1913) - The Lifelong Teacher

Harriet spent almost 40 years teaching English in Alabama. Generations of students learned to read, write and think big in her classroom - service to all mankind, one lesson at a time.

A Fan Tribute

Why We Tell Their Stories

Sixteen young women, mostly teachers, built something that outlasted them all. That is the whole lesson: you do not have to be grown or famous to start something that matters.
