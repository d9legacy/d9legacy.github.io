The History of AKA

The First of Them All

Nine young women in 1908 started the very first Black Greek-letter sorority.
The Beginning

Nine Women, One Legendary Idea

On January 15, 1908, a brilliant Howard University student named Ethel Hedgeman Lyle and eight of her classmates founded Alpha Kappa Alpha - the first Greek-letter sorority ever started by African American women. In 1913, seven more women made sure the sorority would last forever by legally incorporating it. Together, the founders and incorporators built something that now stretches across the whole world.

The Timeline

AKA Through the Years

1908

The First Sorority

Ethel Hedgeman Lyle and eight classmates found Alpha Kappa Alpha at Howard University - the first African American Greek-letter sorority in history.

1913

Incorporated Forever

Seven incorporators - led by Norma Elizabeth Boyd - legally incorporate the sorority on January 29, 1913, so it can grow and last.

1910s - 1940s

Service Takes Root

AKAs open health clinics in the Mississippi Delta, fight for education, and support soldiers and families through two world wars.

1950s - 1960s

Civil Rights Era

AKA women like Coretta Scott King and Rosa Parks stand at the heart of the movement, while chapters raise money for the cause.

1980s - 2000s

Global Reach

The sorority grows to over a thousand chapters worldwide, awarding millions in scholarships and leading health and education programs.

2008 - Today

Over a Century Strong

AKA celebrates 100+ years, sends a member to the Vice Presidency of the United States (Kamala Harris, 2021), and keeps serving “all mankind” - with our D9Legacy chapter along for the ride.

Why It Matters

Still First

Being first means everyone watches how you carry yourself - and for 118 years, AKAs have carried the pink and green with grace. The ivy keeps climbing, and our D9Legacy chapter climbs with it.
