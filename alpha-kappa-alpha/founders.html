The Founders of AKA

Founders & Incorporators

Nine founders in 1908, seven incorporators in 1913 - the sixteen women behind the first.
The Originals

Meet the Sixteen

&

Ethel Hedgeman Lyle

Founder (1908)

The dreamer who started it all. She imagined a sisterhood of high-achieving women - and built it while still a student.

&

Anna Easter Brown

Founder (1908)

An honor student and teacher who spent 50 years educating young people in New Jersey.

&

Beulah Elizabeth Burke

Founder (1908)

A teacher and principal who also studied home economics - she believed practical skills change lives.

&

Lillie Burke

Founder (1908)

Beulah’s sister and a beloved English teacher in Washington, D.C. schools.

&

Marjorie Hill

Founder (1908)

One of the youngest founders - a teacher who carried AKA’s ideals into her classroom.

&

Margaret Flagg Holmes

Founder (1908)

A teacher and librarian who helped keep the young sorority’s records and history.

&

Lavinia Norman

Founder (1908)

A teacher for over 40 years - she taught English, Latin, French and drama.

&

Lucy Diggs Slowe

Founder (1908)

A tennis champion AND the first Dean of Women at Howard University - brains and backhands.

&

Marie Woolfolk Taylor

Founder (1908)

A community leader in Atlanta who fought for better housing and schools her whole life.

&

Norma Elizabeth Boyd

Incorporator (1913)

Led the 1913 incorporation and later served as international president - the guardian of AKA’s future.

&

Ethel Jones Mowbray

Incorporator (1913)

A mathematician and teacher who served as the sorority’s second international president.

&

Alice P. Murray

Incorporator (1913)

A gifted musician and teacher who helped write the sorority’s early constitution.

&

Sarah Meriweather Nutter

Incorporator (1913)

An English teacher and inspector of schools who carried AKA’s standards into classrooms.

&

Joanna Berry Shields

Incorporator (1913)

A teacher and social worker remembered for her warmth and steady leadership.

&

Carrie Snowden

Incorporator (1913)

A teacher and epistoleus (record keeper) who helped preserve AKA’s earliest story.

&

Harriet Josephine Terry

Incorporator (1913)

An English teacher for over 30 years and one of the last surviving incorporators.

The nine founders started AKA in 1908 as Howard students; the seven incorporators made it permanent in 1913. All sixteen are honored every Founders Day.
