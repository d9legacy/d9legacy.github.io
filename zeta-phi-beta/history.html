The History of Zeta

The Finer Sisterhood

Five Howard students in 1920 founded a sorority built on finer womanhood.
The Beginning

Five Pearls, One Shining Idea

On January 16, 1920, five young women at Howard University founded Zeta Phi Beta. They believed a sorority should be judged by what it does for others - scholarship, service, sisterhood and something they called finer womanhood. They also made history by joining hands with Phi Beta Sigma as brother and sister organizations - the first bond of its kind in the Divine Nine.

The Timeline

Zeta Through the Years

1920

The Five Pearls

Arizona Cleaver Stemons, Pearl Anna Neal, Myrtle Tyler Faithful, Viola Tyler Goings and Fannie Pettie Watts found Zeta Phi Beta at Howard University on January 16.

1920s - 1930s

Firsts Keep Coming

Zeta becomes the first sorority constitutionally bound to a fraternity (Phi Beta Sigma), the first to charter international chapters, and home to author Zora Neale Hurston.

1940s - 1960s

Service Grows

Chapters spread across America; Zetas teach, nurse, organize and serve through war years and into the civil rights era.

1970s

The Stork’s Nest

Zeta launches the Stork’s Nest with the March of Dimes - helping moms-to-be get care and healthy starts for their babies.

2000s

Z-HOPE

Zetas Helping Other People Excel brings health, education and elder-care programs to neighborhoods everywhere.

2020s - Today

Finer at 106

Under re-elected International President Dr. Stacie NC Grant (2026), Zetas answer crises like the maternal health emergency with global days of service. Finer womanhood, still in action.

Why It Matters

Still Finer

Over 106 years, the five Pearls’ idea grew into a worldwide sisterhood - still proving that when women lift each other, everybody rises. The D9Legacy chapter is finer for having Zeta in it.
