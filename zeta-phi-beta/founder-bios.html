The Real Women

The Five Pearls

Zeta Phi Beta was founded on January 16, 1920, at Howard University by five young women the world now calls the Five Pearls. They dared to build a new kind of sisterhood - one based on Scholarship, Service, Sisterhood and Finer Womanhood. These biographies are our kid-sized fan tribute to the five.

1920

The Five Pearls

Ζ

Arizona Cleaver Stemons

Founder (1920) - The First President

The one who gathered the pearls. Arizona dreamed of a sorority that would do things differently, talked it over with her friends, and became the new sisterhood's first president. Big dreams, followed by big doing.

Ζ

Pearl Anna Neal

Founder (1920) - The Scholar

Her name really was Pearl - how perfect is that? Pearl loved learning and music, and she helped give the young sorority its heart for scholarship from the very first day.

Ζ

Myrtle Tyler Faithful

Founder (1920) - The Faithful Sister

One of the two Tyler sisters among the Five Pearls. True to her name, Myrtle stayed faithful to the sisterhood she helped start, and her family stayed in the Zeta story for generations.

Ζ

Viola Tyler Goings

Founder (1920) - The Sister's Sister

Myrtle's sister and the second Tyler at the founding table. Viola proves the best part of sisterhood: sometimes your real sister joins too.

Ζ

Fannie Pettie Watts

Founder (1920) - The Teacher

Fannie loved teaching and devoted her life to it. She helped make sure the new sorority stood for service from the very beginning - not just fun, but real help for real people.

Why We Tell Their Stories

Five young women started a sisterhood that grew to more than 125,000 members in over 800 chapters around the world. A pearl starts tiny and becomes treasure - so did they.
