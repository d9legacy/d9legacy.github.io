Divine Nine History

How nine Black Greek-letter organizations grew into one powerful family.

The Real Story

Why the Divine Nine Exists

This page tells real history. On other parts of D9Legacy you will meet pretend characters like Jaylee D. Strong - those stories are made up and we always say so. This page is the true stuff.

It started with being left out

In the early 1900s, Black college students were not allowed to join the white fraternities and sororities on their campuses. So they built their own - organizations about brotherhood, sisterhood, scholarship and lifting up their communities. In 1906 at Cornell University, Alpha Phi Alpha became the first.

Howard University, the heart of the D9

Five of the nine organizations were founded at Howard University in Washington, D.C.: Alpha Kappa Alpha (1908), Omega Psi Phi (1911), Delta Sigma Theta (1913), Phi Beta Sigma (1914) and Zeta Phi Beta (1920). Kappa Alpha Psi started at Indiana University, Sigma Gamma Rho at Butler University, and Iota Phi Theta at Morgan State.

The Council of Nine

In 1930 at Howard University, the organizations formed the National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC) - the group that ties all nine together as one family, the Divine Nine.

Service that changed the country

D9 members marched for civil rights, opened schools and scholarships, registered voters and led movements. Members include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. (Alpha Phi Alpha), Vice President Kamala Harris (Alpha Kappa Alpha) and Michael Jordan (Omega Psi Phi).

The youngest, Iota

In 1963, during the Civil Rights Movement, twelve men at Morgan State founded Iota Phi Theta - proof the family keeps growing.
