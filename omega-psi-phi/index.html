The History of Omega

How It All Began

On the evening of November 17, 1911, inside the Science Building at Howard University in Washington, D.C., three students and their teacher started something special. Omega Psi Phi became the very first fraternity founded at a historically Black college - and brothers have been lifting as they climb ever since.

The Interest Rule Book

Official Rules for the Completion and Submission of the Interest Sheet

Guidelines for the completion and submission of the Interest Sheet are specified below. An applicant for membership in the D9Legacy Omega Chapter is not voted upon; however, each applicant must meet every rule in this book. This rule book is issued by order of the Chapter Owner, under Josh, the founder of D9Legacy. The Owner reads every sheet personally.

  1. One sheet per person. Each applicant shall complete and submit only one Interest Sheet, written by the applicant. Sheets sent by friends on an applicant’s behalf, or many sheets from one person, are set aside - they only make a pile.
  2. Tell the truth. The applicant gives real answers only. The Owner reads every single sheet, and made-up answers are easy to spot. Falsification of any information on the Interest Sheet ends the application at once.
  3. Be kind, always. Mean words, teasing, or leaving people out - on a sheet, at school, or anywhere - ends an application right there. Omegas lift people up.
  4. Use a real first name and grade. Every sheet carries the applicant’s real first name and grade. The chapter cannot write a letter to a mystery person.
  5. Phone numbers. An applicant shares a phone number only in fourth (4th) grade or higher, and only the applicant’s own - never a friend’s, and never a grown-up’s without asking first.
  6. Omegas are helpers. On the sheet, each applicant names one way of having helped somebody - at school, at home, or anywhere. Uplift starts small.
  7. Keep up schoolwork. Scholarship is one of Omega’s cardinal principles. An applicant keeps up with class and brings a best effort to school first.
  8. Be patient after sending. Every sheet goes to “pending” while the Owner reads it. Reading takes time - pending means a sheet is being taken seriously.
  9. No begging for a letter. Letters come after review, never before. Asking over and over does not move an applicant up the list.
  10. A “not yet” is not a “never.” If a letter says try again, the applicant fixes what it asks and sends a new sheet when ready.
  11. Keep chapter things in the chapter. What is shared in the chapter inbox stays with the chapter. That is how brotherhood works.
  12. Remember what every sheet and letter says: With Jesus Christ. Treat people like it matters, because it does.

Follow these rules, send your sheet, and you are officially interested. The rest is up to your answers - and a little perseverance.

Issued under Josh, Founder of D9Legacy
