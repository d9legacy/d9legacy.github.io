The History of Sigma

Brotherhood For Everybody

Three Howard students in 1914 built a fraternity on inclusion and service.
The Beginning

Three Men, One Open Door

On January 9, 1914, three young men at Howard University - A. Langston Taylor, Leonard F. Morse and Charles I. Brown - founded Phi Beta Sigma. They had seen clubs that kept people out, and they wanted the opposite: a brotherhood where every member truly belonged, built to serve all humanity. A few years later they helped five young women found Zeta Phi Beta, and the two families have been bound together ever since.

The Timeline

Sigma Through the Years

1914

The Three Founders

Taylor, Morse and Brown found Phi Beta Sigma at Howard University on January 9 - a fraternity built on inclusion: brotherhood, scholarship, service.

1919 - 1920

A Sister Is Born

Sigma brothers help five Howard women found Zeta Phi Beta - and the two organizations bind themselves together as family. A Greek-life first.

1920s - 1940s

Bigger and Better Business

Sigma launches its Bigger and Better Business program to grow Black-owned businesses - and brother James Weldon Johnson pens the Black national anthem.

1950s - 1960s

The Movement

Brothers like John Lewis face down fire hoses and jail cells for freedom, marching from Selma to Washington.

1980s - 2000s

Three Pillars

Sigma’s programs settle into three big pushes: Bigger and Better Business, Education, and Social Action.

2014 - Today

Over a Century of Service

Sigma celebrates 100+ years, elects Hon. Bro. David A. Turner as its 37th International President, and keeps serving - with Zeta by its side.

Why It Matters

Still Serving Humanity

Three men opened a door in 1914 and never closed it. 112 years later, Sigma brothers are still proving that inclusion plus service equals progress - and our D9Legacy chapter holds that same door open.
