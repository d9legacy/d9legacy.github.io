The Real Men

Three Founders, One Brotherhood

Phi Beta Sigma was founded on January 9, 1914, at Howard University by three young men who wanted a fraternity built on Brotherhood, Scholarship and Service - one that would include people, not leave them out. These biographies are our kid-sized fan tribute to the three.

1914

The Three Founders

Σ

A. Langston Taylor

Founder (1914) - The Dreamer

The one with the idea. Langston Taylor imagined a fraternity whose whole life would be service to the community - and he became its first president. He is remembered as the father of Phi Beta Sigma.

Σ

Leonard F. Morse

Founder (1914) - The Scholar

A brilliant student who helped shape the new fraternity's ideals and its early documents. He later became a college professor, spending his life exactly the way Sigma teaches: learning, then giving it back.

Σ

Charles I. Brown

Founder (1914) - The Builder

The third man at the table, helping turn two friends' idea into a real brotherhood. Three names, one fraternity - and more than a century of brothers since.

A Special Bond

Brother and Sister

Phi Beta Sigma has a bond no other fraternity has: Zeta Phi Beta Sorority is its constitutionally bound sister organization. The two were built as family from the start - the only brother-sister pair in the Divine Nine. Brotherhood and sisterhood, on purpose, forever.
