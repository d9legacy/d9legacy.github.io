The Real Women

Seven Teachers, One Sisterhood

Sigma Gamma Rho was organized on November 12, 1922, in Indianapolis, Indiana, by seven young educators - all of them schoolteachers. It is the only sorority of the Divine Nine founded at a mostly white school, Butler University, which made their courage even bigger. These biographies are our kid-sized fan tribute to the seven.

1922

The Seven Founders

Σ

Mary Lou Allison Gardner Little

Founder (1922) - The First President

The one who dreamed the dream first. Mary Lou imagined a brand-new sisterhood of teachers who would lift up their whole community - and then she led it, becoming the sorority's very first Grand Basileus (that means national president).

Σ

Dorothy Hanley Whiteside

Founder (1922) - The Encourager

An Indianapolis teacher who said yes when the idea was brand new and unproven. Every big thing starts with somebody willing to believe in it on day one, and Dorothy was one of those somebodies.

Σ

Vivian Irene White Marbury

Founder (1922) - The Organizer

A teacher who loved helping younger girls grow. Sisters remember her for inspiring the sorority's clubs for teen girls - the Rhoers - so the sisterhood could reach the next generation early.

Σ

Nannie Mae Gahn Johnson

Founder (1922) - The Steady One

One of the seven teachers who kept showing up. Building something new is hard, and Nannie Mae helped the young sorority hold together through its very first years.

Σ

Hattie Mae Annette Dulin Redford

Founder (1922) - The Believer

A teacher who believed young women could lead real change - not someday, but right then. Hattie helped turn that belief into a sisterhood that is still serving more than a century later.

Σ

Bessie Mae Downey Rhoades Martin

Founder (1922) - The Builder

One of the seven who did the unglamorous work of building: meetings, plans, and keeping sisters connected. Every strong house needs builders, and Bessie Mae was one of Sigma's first.

Σ

Cubena McClure

Founder (1922) - The Creative

A teacher remembered for helping shape how the young sorority looked and felt in its earliest days. Her part in the founding is still honored by sisters today.

A Fan Tribute

Why We Tell Their Stories

Seven teachers started a sisterhood that grew to more than 100,000 members around the world. The lesson is simple: teachers change the world, and so can kids who work together.
