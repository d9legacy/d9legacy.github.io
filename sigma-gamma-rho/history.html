The History of Sigma

Seven Teachers, Greater Progress

Founded at Butler University in 1922 - the only D9 sorority born at a predominantly white school.
The Beginning

A Sisterhood of Teachers

On November 12, 1922, seven young schoolteachers at Butler University in Indianapolis founded Sigma Gamma Rho. It was 1922 - a hard time to be a Black woman in America, and an even harder place to start a sorority. But these seven educators believed service and education could change everything. They were right: over 100 years later, their sisterhood spans the globe.

The Timeline

Sigma Through the Years

1922

Seven Teachers

Seven educators - Little, Whiteside, Marbury, Johnson, Redford, Martin and McClure - found Sigma Gamma Rho at Butler University on November 12.

1920s - 1940s

Teaching Service

The sorority grows across the country, launching national projects like the Vocational Guidance Center and fighting for Black teachers’ rights.

1950s - 1960s

Civil Rights & Classrooms

Sigmas teach, organize and march - including soror Hattie McDaniel blazing trails in Hollywood and chapters supporting the movement.

1980s - 2000s

Programs for Families

Project Reassurance supports healthy families, Operation BigBookBag fills backpacks with school supplies, and the Mwanamugimu Essay Contest gets kids writing.

2016

Swim 1922

Sigma partners with USA Swimming to teach water safety - because too many Black kids never learn to swim. Lessons that literally save lives.

2022 - Today

A Century of Service

The sorority celebrates 100 years, signs education partnerships like its 2026 agreement with Western Governors University, and inducts new honorary members at the 61st Boule. Greater service, greater progress.

Why It Matters

Still Teaching

Seven teachers started Sigma to serve, and every Sigma since has kept the lesson going. The D9Legacy chapter studies their example: serve greater, progress greater.
