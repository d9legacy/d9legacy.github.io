The History of Kappa

Born to Achieve

Ten young men at Indiana University founded Kappa on January 5, 1911.
The Beginning

Ten Dreamers in Bloomington

On January 5, 1911, ten young men at Indiana University in Bloomington founded the fraternity we now call Kappa Alpha Psi. Their leader, Elder Watson Diggs, was known as “the Dreamer.” At a school where they weren’t always welcome, these ten built a brotherhood around one idea: achievement in every field of human endeavor. First called Kappa Alpha Nu, the fraternity took its famous name in 1915 - and the Nupes have been achieving ever since.

The Timeline

Kappa Through the Years

1911

The Ten Founders

Elder Watson Diggs and nine brothers found Kappa Alpha Nu at Indiana University on January 5 - a brotherhood for achievement.

1915

A New Name

The fraternity becomes Kappa Alpha Psi - the name known around the world today.

1922

Guide Right Begins

Kappa launches Guide Right, its national mentoring program for young people - still running over 100 years later.

1930s - 1960s

Achievers Rise

Brothers break barriers in every field - including a young Arthur Ashe, who would conquer the tennis world.

1970s - 2000s

Kappa Leagues

Kappa League youth clubs spread across America, training young men in leadership and college prep.

2011 - Today

115 Years of Achievement

Kappa celebrates its centennial in 2011, keeps growing worldwide, and under 35th Grand Polemarch Jimmy McMikle keeps chasing achievement in every field.

Still Achieving
