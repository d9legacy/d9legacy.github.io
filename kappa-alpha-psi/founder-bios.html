The Real Men

Ten Dreamers, One Brotherhood

Kappa Alpha Psi began on January 5, 1911, when ten young Black men at Indiana University in Bloomington started a brotherhood of their own at a time when most campus clubs would not welcome them. The fraternity's first name was Kappa Alpha Nu - in 1915 it became Kappa Alpha Psi, the name the whole world knows today. These biographies are our kid-sized fan tribute to the ten founders, known forever as The Dreamers.

1911

The Ten Founders

&

Elder Watson Diggs

Founder - The Dreamer

The founder of founders. Elder Diggs dreamed up the fraternity, brought the other nine together, and led it as its very first Grand Polemarch - that is Kappa's special word for national president. He spent his working life as a teacher and school principal in Indiana, proving his dream was always about lifting young people up.

&

Ezra D. Alexander

Founder - The Doctor

The steady hand of the ten. Ezra served as the young fraternity's first secretary, writing everything down so Kappa's story would never be lost. He went on to become a medical doctor and spent his career caring for patients back home in Indiana.

&

Byron Kenneth Armstrong

Founder - The Scholar

Brains on an international level. Byron studied at Columbia University in New York and at Oxford University in England - one of the greatest schools on Earth - then spent his life teaching and leading at universities. A Dreamer who proved books can take you anywhere.

&

Henry Tourner Asher

Founder - The Educator

Henry believed the classroom was where you change the world. He became a teacher and then a principal, spending his whole life helping young people learn, grow and aim higher than anyone told them they could.

&

Marcus Peter Blakemore

Founder - The Spirit

Marcus gave young Kappa much of its heart. He became a minister and a teacher - a man of faith who spent his life guiding people, in church and in the classroom alike.

&

Paul Waymond Caine

Founder - The Host

Every brotherhood needs somebody who keeps everyone fed and friendly, and that was Paul. He became a chef and a businessman, famous among the founders for his cooking and his kindness. The brother who made Kappa feel like family.

&

George Wesley Edmonds

Founder - The Guide

George helped steer the fraternity's very first steps in Bloomington, keeping the dream on track when it was still small and fragile. He became a teacher, guiding young people the same careful way he guided his fraternity.

&

Guy Levis Grant

Founder - The Pharmacist

Guy became a pharmacist and businessman in Indianapolis, and he never stopped carrying Kappa's banner. Brothers like Guy made sure the fraternity kept growing long after the college years were over.

&

Edward Giles Irvin

Founder - The Storyteller

The youngest of the ten. Edward became a reporter and newspaper editor - a professional storyteller. Fitting, because the story he helped start in 1911 is still being told more than a century later.

&

John Milton Lee

Founder - The Soldier-Scholar

John served his country as a soldier and hit the books as a scholar. He is remembered as one of Kappa's earliest achievers - a living picture of the fraternity's motto, achievement in every field of human endeavor.
