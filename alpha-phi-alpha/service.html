Servants of All

Helping Is the Whole Point

Alpha Phi Alpha's motto says it all: “First of All, Servants of All, We Shall Transcend All.” Servants of ALL. Not some. All. Since 1906 that has meant teaching, marching, voting drives and lifting up young people. Here is what that looks like - the real history and our chapter's own kid-sized version.

The Real Legacy

A History of Helping

1922

Go-to-High-School, Go-to-College

Way back in the 1920s, Alpha launched a national campaign with one simple message for young people: stay in school, then go further. At a time when many Black kids were pushed out of classrooms, Alphas were pulling them in.

1930s

A Voteless People Is a Hopeless People

Alpha's famous voting campaign taught Black Americans why registering and voting matters - decades before the big civil rights laws. A voteless people is a hopeless people: eight words that helped change history.

1950s - 1960s

Leading the Movement

The Civil Rights Movement had Alphas at its heart - Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. and Thurgood Marshall, the lawyer who won Brown v. Board of Education and became the first Black Supreme Court Justice, were both Alpha men.

Today

Project Alpha

With the March of Dimes, the real fraternity runs Project Alpha, teaching young men about responsibility, respect and good decisions - service aimed straight at the next generation.

Our Chapter's Initiatives

Gold Standard Tutoring

Homework help, Alpha style

Members pair up with younger kids at Magnolia Crown for weekly homework help. Go to high school, go to college - it starts with tonight's math worksheet.

Black & Gold Clean-Up

Service you can see

Once a month the chapter picks up the campus and the neighborhood in black and gold. Servants of all, brooms in hand.

Little Voices Day

Every kid gets heard

Once a semester, members help younger students practice speaking up - sharing ideas, asking questions and using their voices. A voiceless people is a hopeless people, so we practice early.
