The History of Alpha

First of All

Seven students at Cornell University started something the world had never seen.
The Beginning

Seven Jewels, One First

On December 4, 1906, seven students at Cornell University in Ithaca, New York founded Alpha Phi Alpha - the first intercollegiate fraternity for African Americans. At a mostly white school in the early 1900s, these seven young men studied together, supported each other, and built a brotherhood that would open the door for every Black Greek organization after them. That’s why they’re called the Seven Jewels.

The Timeline

Alpha Through the Years

1905 - 1906

The Social Study Club

Black students at Cornell start meeting to study and support each other. On December 4, 1906, they become Alpha Phi Alpha - the first.

1907 - 1920s

The Door Opens

New chapters form - including at Howard University - and Alpha proves Black college students can build organizations that last.

1930s - 1940s

Education for All

The fraternity launches “Go to High School, Go to College” and “A Voteless People Is a Hopeless People” - pushing kids to school and citizens to the ballot box.

1950s - 1960s

The Movement

Brother Martin Luther King Jr. leads the civil rights movement, Thurgood Marshall becomes the first Black Supreme Court Justice (1967), and Alphas are everywhere freedom is fought for.

1970s - 2000s

Building Leaders

Project Alpha teaches young men about responsibility with the March of Dimes, and the fraternity keeps growing worldwide.

2006 - Today

120 Years First

Alpha celebrates its centennial in 2006 and keeps leading - still first, still serving, still transcending. Our D9Legacy chapter proudly follows.

Why It Matters

Still First

Every Black Greek-letter organization - including every site in the D9Legacy universe - exists because seven students at Cornell went first. That’s not just history. That’s a responsibility Alpha still carries.
